Prepared voice patterns · open to compare
  • Short to medium sentences. Starts with what the reader needs to do. Plain verbs (need, read, makes). Names who decides. Friendly but not chatty. No exclamation marks.
  • Leads with counts tied to a time frame (last cycle). States limits openly. One reason sentence at the end, no slogan. Uses 'we' for the organization and 'students' instead of labels like 'at-risk' or 'underprivileged'.

    For the coach
    1. Let the learner keep the keyboard and choose a case.
    2. Use the fictional samples to practice. Real organization samples are not needed in this exercise.
    3. Ask what the learner noticed in the source facts before opening a prepared answer.
    4. Have the learner write the reader’s next step and two or three voice rules they can explain.
    5. If they are stuck, open the prepared guide together. Ask which rule they would keep and why.
    6. Try the same voice rules on another case. Discuss what changes with the audience and what stays true.
